Short answer

Maternity leave in UAE private-sector companies is 60 days: the first 45 days at full wage and the next 15 days at half wage. The rule is Article 30 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 Regarding the Regulation of Employment Relationships. The same article adds up to 45 unpaid days for illness linked to pregnancy or childbirth, 30 fully paid days where a child who is sick or has a disability needs constant care, and nursing breaks for six months. Either parent also gets 5 working days of paid parental leave (Article 32). The DIFC and ADGM have their own rules (65 working days, with conditions), and government employees are under separate HR laws.

The federal law covers establishments, employers and workers in the UAE private sector. It does not cover employees of federal and local government entities, the armed forces, police and security, or domestic workers (Article 3 of the Decree-Law). The DIFC and ADGM apply their own employment laws.

Other emirates' governments are covered on the u.ae page on leave in the government sector.

How maternity leave in UAE private-sector companies works

Article 30 of the Decree-Law sets these rules:

  • Length and pay: 60 days (45 at full wage, then 15 at half wage), counted as days, not working days.
  • Start: the employer must grant the leave at the worker's request from the last day of the month before the month of the expected birth, proven by a certificate from a medical entity. The u.ae leave page puts this as up to 30 days before the expected delivery date.
  • Eligibility: the delivery must take place six months or more into the pregnancy; a stillbirth or the child's death after birth does not remove the leave. Article 30 sets no minimum length of service.
  • Second job: if she works for another employer during the leave, the original employer may withhold or recover the leave wage.
  • Other leave: maternity leave does not reduce her right to other leaves.

Maternity leave pay is calculated on the "wage", which Article 1 defines as the basic wage plus the cash allowances and benefits in kind allocated under the contract. How commission and variable items count depends on the contract.

These are minimum rights. A contract term or policy that gives less is void, and an employer may give more (Article 65).

The 45 unpaid days do not count towards the service period used for end-of-service benefits or pension contributions.

Ordinary sick leave is separate (Article 31). Parental, annual and unpaid leave may be combined under Article 21 of the Executive Regulations (Cabinet Resolution No. 1 of 2022, in the same document). Any other unpaid leave needs the employer's approval (Article 33).

Nursing breaks and parental leave after the birth

After her return, the worker is entitled to one or two nursing breaks a day, not exceeding one hour in total, for up to six months from the delivery date (Article 30(9)). The u.ae leave page describes these breaks as fully paid.

Federal private-sector law has no separate paternity leave. Under that law, parental leave in the UAE is 5 working days of paid leave for the father or the mother, taken in one block or in parts within six months of the birth (Article 32(1)(b)). The worker must submit proof of the birth (Article 21 of the Executive Regulations).

What the law says about dismissal and penalties

An employer may not terminate a worker's service, or give her notice, because of pregnancy, maternity leave or absence allowed by Article 30 (Article 30(8) of the Decree-Law). Article 4 separately prohibits discrimination on the basis of sex.

As worded, the ban concerns the reason for dismissal. If the contract is ended for another lawful reason while she is on leave, the notice period starts only on the day after her scheduled return, unless both parties agree otherwise (Article 35). Whether a reason is unrelated to pregnancy is a question of fact.

A breach of a provision that has no specific penalty carries a fine of AED 5,000 to AED 1,000,000 (Article 63).

How the DIFC and ADGM rules differ

In the DIFC, the Employment Law, DIFC Law No. 2 of 2019 (Consolidated Version No. 5, July 2025) gives up to 65 work days. Maternity pay is due only if the employee has 12 months' continuous employment, gives written notice of the pregnancy at least 8 weeks before the expected week of childbirth, provides a medical certificate and gives 21 days' written notice of the start date (Article 37). Adoption of a child under five is covered. Nursing breaks of at least one hour a day run for six months where the working day exceeds six hours (Article 26). Paternity leave is up to 5 work days, with conditions (Article 39). Dismissal, or a change of terms without her written consent, because of pregnancy is prohibited (Article 40).

In ADGM, the Employment Regulations 2024 apply from 1 April 2025, according to the ADGM Employment Affairs Office. Its guidance sets out 65 working days with the same pay split and the same 12-month and notice conditions. Nursing breaks run for nine months after the birth where the working day exceeds six hours, and paternity leave is 5 working days within two months. A consultation opened on 16 November 2026 proposes changes to maternity and paternity leave; none is law yet.

Checklist for employers handling a maternity leave request

  • Confirm which law covers the employing entity: federal, DIFC or ADGM. Steps 2 to 8 follow the federal law.
  • Read the leave clauses in the employment contract and HR policy.
  • Ask for the medical certificate and record the start date in writing.
  • Pay 45 days at full wage and 15 at half wage, and check what "wage" covers under the contract.
  • Record extra leave separately: 45 unpaid days for illness, 30 paid plus 30 unpaid days for a sick child.
  • Agree the nursing-break schedule for the six months after delivery.
  • Grant 5 working days of parental leave to either parent on proof of birth.
  • Do not give notice because of pregnancy or leave. For any other termination during her leave, record the reason and count notice from the day after her scheduled return.
  • In the DIFC and ADGM, check the 12-month and notice conditions before confirming pay.

FAQ

How many days is maternity leave in the UAE private sector?

It is 60 days under Article 30 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021. The u.ae leave page, updated on 14 October 2026, states the same.

Is maternity leave fully paid in the UAE?

Under federal private-sector law, only in part. The first 45 days are at full wage and the next 15 at half wage. The 45 extra days for illness are unpaid; the 30 days for a sick child are at full pay (Article 30 of the Decree-Law).

Is there paternity leave in the UAE?

Under federal private-sector law, a father uses parental leave: 5 paid working days within six months of the birth (Article 32). The DIFC and ADGM each provide 5 working days of paternity leave on their own conditions.

Do the same rules apply in every emirate?

For private employers under federal law, yes: maternity leave in Dubai, Abu Dhabi or Sharjah is the same 60 days. The exceptions are DIFC and ADGM employers, and government employees, who follow their own government's HR law.

Do Emirati mothers in private companies get longer leave?

The federal entitlement is the same for every nationality. In Abu Dhabi, the Abu Dhabi Social Support Authority announced on 27 August 2024 salary support for Emirati mothers who hold an Abu Dhabi family book, allowing up to 90 days of paid maternity leave. It needs the employer's no-objection certificate. Check the current terms with the authority.
