Why banking and finance companies in the UAE need ongoing legal support

The central bank law was replaced in 2025. Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025 Regarding the Central Bank, Regulation of Financial Institutions and Activities, and Insurance Business took effect on 16 September 2025. It repealed the 2018 central bank law (No. 14 of 2018) and the 2023 insurance law (No. 48 of 2023). Searches for UAE banking law, banking control law or new banking laws now lead to it. Article 61 lists the activities that need a Central Bank licence, among them taking deposits, credit and funding facilities, open finance, currency exchange and money transfer, virtual-asset payment services, stored value and retail payments, and arranging or marketing these. Article 62 applies the licensing regime "regardless of the medium, technology, or form employed". Article 170 punishes unlicensed activity with imprisonment and a fine of AED 50,000 to AED 500 million, or either of the two penalties.

The law has transition rules. Article 184 allows those subject to the law one year from its entry into force to reconcile their position and lets the Central Bank's board extend that period; the Central Bank's current decisions show whether it has. Under Article 183, regulations issued under the 2018 law stay in force until replaced.

Capital markets have a new regulator. On 1 January 2026 the Capital Market Authority (CMA) replaced the Securities and Commodities Authority under Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2025. Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2025 Regarding the Regulation of Capital Market took effect the same day. Its Article 83 provides one year from that date for those it covers to regularise their status and allows the CMA board to extend it, so check the CMA's current decisions before relying on a date.

The AML law was replaced. Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 on anti-money laundering, terrorism financing and proliferation financing has applied since 14 October 2025 and repealed Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018. Its Executive Regulations are Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025, in force since 14 December 2025.

There are three regimes. The federal Central Bank law does not apply in the financial free zones (Article 2). Those zones exist under Federal Law No. 8 of 2004, which disapplies federal civil and commercial laws there but keeps other federal law, anti-money laundering law included (Article 3). In the DIFC, the DFSA regulates financial services conducted in or from the centre under the Regulatory Law, DIFC Law No. 1 of 2004. In ADGM, the FSRA applies the Financial Services and Markets Regulations 2015. A search for offshore banking law in the UAE often leads to these two zones, which are UAE territory, not offshore jurisdictions.

The legal work that comes up every month

  • Borrowing and lending. Term sheets, facility agreements, amendments, waivers and real estate finance documents, whether the field is corporate finance law or project finance law.
  • Guarantees and security. Corporate and bank guarantees, pledges and assignments. Article 150 of the Central Bank law requires licensed institutions to hold adequate guarantees for facilities to natural persons and sole proprietorships, and bars their claims where they have not.
  • Bank, customer and partner contracts. Account, cash-management and trade-finance terms from banks; lending and wallet terms; merchant, agent and outsourcing agreements.
  • Confidentiality. Article 147 of the Central Bank law works as the bank secrecy law for licensed institutions: customer data may be disclosed only with consent or in the cases it lists.
  • Research memos. How Article 61 reads against a product, how a cross-border facility is treated (a question of international banking law), which regime applies in the DIFC or ADGM.

Checklist before you sign a legal retainer

  • The legal retainer agreement lists the included work in writing.
  • Exclusions are stated, for example onshore court litigation, tax advisory or compliance audits.
  • The fee is fixed, VAT is shown separately, and the response time is stated.
  • A lawyer reviews every document.
  • The provider's licence and licensing authority are stated.
  • Confidentiality and conflicts are covered.
  • Limits are clear: no investment advice, no promise of a regulator's approval.
  • Separately scoped work (company registration, arbitration, M&A) has its own quote, and cancellation terms are stated.

FAQ

What is a retainer fee for a lawyer?

A recurring fee for agreed legal work over a period, instead of a bill per hour. Under the REGULEX subscription it is a fixed monthly fee from USD 990 plus 5% VAT.

What are the new banking laws in the UAE?

The main one is Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025 on the Central Bank, with the 2025 AML law and the two 2025 capital market laws cited above.

What is securities law in the UAE?

Onshore, the main statute is Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2025, applied by the CMA. It covers financial products dealt in within the State and activities such as brokerage, portfolio management, promotion and financial advisory (Articles 2 and 3).

How does Sharia law apply in UAE banking?

Islamic financial institutions must have an Internal Shari'ah Supervision Committee, unless the Central Bank exempts them, under Article 75 of the Central Bank law, and the Commercial Transactions Law has a part on their contracts, such as Murabaha and Ijarah. Matters governed by Sharia law, including Islamic finance contracts, are outside the REGULEX subscription.

Does a retainer replace a bank fraud lawyer or financial services litigation lawyers?

No. Litigation before the UAE onshore courts is not included in the REGULEX subscription, and REGULEX does not appear before those courts, so fraud claims and other court cases need separate court representation. A banking lawyer on retainer reviews the contracts and writes a non-binding memo. The same applies to bank attorney, financial services attorney or securities fraud lawyer searches.
