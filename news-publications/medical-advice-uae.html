Can Foreign Doctors Offer Online Medical Advice in the UAE?

May 2025 By Oleg Pikalov, Managing Partner

The rapid expansion of telemedicine across the Middle East has created new opportunities for healthcare providers, yet it has also raised critical questions about regulatory compliance in jurisdictions like the UAE. For foreign physicians seeking to offer medical advice or consultations to UAE-based patients, understanding the applicable regulatory framework is essential. Federal Decree-Law No. 5 of 2019 on the Regulation of Medical Practice governs all forms of medical practice in the Emirates, including remote consultations and telemedicine services.

Licensing Requirements for Telemedicine Providers

The foundational requirement under Federal Decree-Law No. 5 of 2019 is that any individual providing medical services, including advice and consultations, must hold a valid medical license issued by the appropriate health authority. For the Emirate of Dubai, this responsibility falls to the Dubai Health Authority (DHA), while Abu Dhabi is regulated by the Department of Health (DOH). The law explicitly covers all forms of medical practice, including telemedicine, online consultations, and remote diagnosis. A foreign doctor practising telemedicine with UAE residents must therefore first obtain recognition of their credentials and a provisional or permanent medical license from the relevant emirate health authority.

The registration process typically requires the following documentation: a verified copy of the applicant's medical degree and professional qualifications, proof of current registration and good standing in the home country, a criminal background check, and evidence of professional liability insurance. Additionally, many health authorities conduct examinations or competency assessments to ensure the applicant meets UAE standards. The timeline for registration can range from several weeks to several months, depending on the completeness of the application and the specific requirements of the emirate.

Remote Medical Consultations and Regulatory Oversight

Once properly licensed, foreign physicians may conduct remote consultations with UAE patients, subject to ongoing oversight and compliance obligations. The DHA and DOH have issued supplementary guidelines detailing acceptable practices for telemedicine, including requirements for informed consent, patient confidentiality, adequate record-keeping, and secure data transmission. Health authorities expect that all electronic communications with patients are encrypted and that patient records are maintained in compliance with UAE data protection laws, particularly the Federal Law No. 45 of 2021 on Data Protection.

A critical consideration is the scope of practice. Foreign doctors must ensure that the services they provide fall within their approved scope of licence and are appropriate for remote delivery. Certain diagnostic or treatment procedures that require physical examination or in-person assessment cannot be performed remotely. Healthcare providers must maintain proper documentation of all remote consultations, including the patient's consent, the nature of the consultation, advice given, and any recommendations for follow-up care or in-person evaluation.

Penalties for Non-Compliance and Best Practices for Market Entry

Violations of medical practice regulations carry serious consequences. Foreign physicians offering medical advice without proper licensure face substantial fines, confiscation of medical equipment, suspension or revocation of any existing license, and potential criminal prosecution. The law also imposes liability on healthcare facilities or platforms that knowingly facilitate unlicensed practice. Given these risks, foreign healthcare providers are strongly advised to prioritize obtaining proper authorization before commencing any services.

For healthcare entities considering entry into the UAE telemedicine market, a prudent approach includes engaging local legal counsel early to navigate the licensing process, establishing partnerships with licensed UAE healthcare providers or institutions, implementing robust compliance systems to monitor regulatory changes, and obtaining comprehensive professional liability insurance that covers telemedicine services. Platforms facilitating remote consultations should implement technological safeguards to verify the licensing status of practitioners and ensure that all services comply with local regulations. By investing in proper compliance infrastructure, foreign healthcare providers can effectively serve the growing demand for medical expertise in the UAE while mitigating legal and reputational risks.
